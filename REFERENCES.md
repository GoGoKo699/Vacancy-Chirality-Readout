# References and attribution

The results specify a vacancy-return instrument, finite-repulsion chirality reconstruction, and a certified precision separation from arbitrary passive four-mode interferometers with terminal total-site-parity detection. The spin-cycle mechanism, chirality observables, interaction-assisted readout, and permutation measurement have established antecedents. The contribution is the quantitative bound for the declared measurement class and its interacting violation, not the first cyclic or interaction-assisted parity measurement.

## Mathematical and measurement predecessors

**[K22]** K. K. Kesharpu and E. A. Kochetov, *Spin-flip-induced superfluidity in a ring of spinful hard-core bosons*, Physical Review A **106**, 033323 (2022). [arXiv:2209.06149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.06149). The cyclic spin permutation on the closing link and the resulting spin-sector boundary flux underlie the hard-core reduction. The repository specializes this mechanism to an explicit vacancy-return measurement.

**[D12]** A. J. Daley, H. Pichler, J. Schachenmayer, and P. Zoller, *Measuring entanglement growth in quench dynamics of bosons in an optical lattice*, Physical Review Letters **109**, 020505 (2012). [Article](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.109.020505) · [arXiv:1205.1521](https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.1521). The general Fourier protocol reads cyclic permutations from occupations modulo the number of copies. Supplement IV gives a particularly close predecessor: for three copies of hard-core bosons, interactions remain on during the mixing sequence, and occupation parity suffices to distinguish the cyclic eigenstates. Thus using interactions to make cyclic information accessible to parity detection is established. Their qubits are the empty/occupied states of hard-core modes across copies; here they are the internal spins of three particles initially occupying three of four modes. The four-mode passive precision bound below addresses this latter input and detector class.

**[R23]** L. I. Reascos, B. Murta, E. F. Galvão, and J. Fernández-Rossier, *Quantum circuits to measure scalar spin chirality*, Physical Review Research **5**, 043087 (2023). [Article](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevResearch.5.043087) · [arXiv:2306.14804](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14804). This work supplies direct Pauli-string measurements, a controlled-cycle Hadamard test, qutrit phase estimation, and measurement-based chirality-sector preparation. Its normalized observable is $\widehat\chi=4\chi/\sqrt3$. The chirality witness and sector-preparation principle are inherited. The direct Pauli baseline requires no entangling measurement gate.

**[TBN26]** P. Tulewicz, K. Bartkiewicz, and F. Nori, *Spin chirality across quantum state copies detects hidden entanglement*, [arXiv:2605.14515v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14515v1) (2026). Supplement S2 and Table S1 specify controlled-permutation Hadamard tests on two to four copies; S3.3 identifies the spins from different copies in the chirality correlator. This connects chirality correlations to entanglement detection through spectral moments. The multi-copy target and controlled gates differ from estimating a single three-spin input's chirality through spatial mixing and terminal site parity.

**[EW01]** T. Eggeling and R. F. Werner, *Separability properties of tripartite states with $U\otimes U\otimes U$ symmetry*, Physical Review A **63**, 042111 (2001). [arXiv:quant-ph/0010096](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0010096). The collective-unitary commutant is permutation-generated. For three qubits its Hermitian span is five-dimensional; combining it with spatial reflection gives the two-observable finite-repulsion contrast used here.

**[CLMOW14]** E. Chitambar, D. Leung, L. Mancinska, M. Ozols, and A. Winter, *Everything You Always Wanted to Know About LOCC (But Were Afraid to Ask)*, Communications in Mathematical Physics **328**, 303–326 (2014). [Article](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00220-014-1953-9) · [arXiv:1210.4583](https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.4583). Section 2.3 establishes the inclusion of LOCC in separable operations. This supports the operational scope of the per-copy separable-effects benchmark; it does not supply the chirality-specific constant derived here.

**[MP2009]** A. Maurer and M. Pontil, *Empirical Bernstein Bounds and Sample Variance Penalization* (2009). [arXiv:0907.3740](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.3740). Theorem 4 supplies the bounded-variable empirical Bernstein inequality used for held-out reference validation. Two tails and five statistics give the logarithm $\log(20/\delta)$ in the channel-calibration certificate. The reference allocation and preparation-cost calculations here are applications, not new concentration inequalities.

**[GL95]** R. D. Gill and B. Y. Levit, *Applications of the van Trees Inequality: A Bayesian Cramér–Rao Bound*, Bernoulli **1** (1–2), 59–79 (1995). [Article](https://doi.org/10.2307/3318681) · [Author manuscript](https://dspace.library.uu.nl/bitstream/handle/1874/1190/van_trees.pdf?sequence=1). Section 2, Eq. (3), gives the scalar van Trees inequality. The finite biased-estimator benchmark applies it to the chirality submodel with a cosine-squared auxiliary prior and reproduces the integration-by-parts proof.

**[BDL11]** S. Bravyi, D. P. DiVincenzo, and D. Loss, *Schrieffer–Wolff transformation for quantum many-body systems*, Annals of Physics **326**, 2793–2826 (2011). [arXiv:1105.0675](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.0675). This is the background for the optional fixed-time block-elimination interpretation. The quantitative operation bound in this repository instead follows directly from energy conservation and Duhamel’s formula.

**[M17]** A. J. Menssen and collaborators, *Distinguishability and many-particle interference*, Physical Review Letters **118**, 153603 (2017). [Article](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.118.153603) · [arXiv:1609.09804](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.09804). Independently prepared photons in a tritter exhibit spatial-count sensitivity to a collective internal-state triad phase. Their supplementary Eqs. (20)–(21), with $R=r_{12}r_{23}r_{31}$, give

$$
P_{120}=\frac{1-2R\cos(\varphi+\pi/3)}9,
\qquad
P_{021}=\frac{1-2R\cos(\varphi-\pi/3)}9.
$$

Subtracting these expressions yields

$$
P_{120}-P_{021}=\frac{2\sqrt3}{9}R\sin\varphi.
$$

This algebraic consequence explicitly credits a precedent for **signed** internal-state information in spatial interference. The broad conversion of internal-state information into spatial counts is established prior art. This attribution supersedes the narrower “cosine-type” characterization in the preserved source record.

**[N26]** L. Novo, M. Robbio, E. F. Galvão, and N. J. Cerf, *Native linear-optical protocol for efficient multivariate trace estimation*, [arXiv:2601.14204](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14204) (2026). Equations (3)–(12) diagonalize the cyclic permutation with Fourier interferometry and internal-state-insensitive particle counts. Theorem 1 explicitly permits entangled inputs. Thus arbitrary correlated inputs alone do not distinguish the present protocol. The count statistic is $f(\mathbf S)=\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}jS_j\pmod M$. At $M=3$, occupations $(3,0,0)$ and $(1,2,0)$ have identical parity $(1,0,0)$ but $f=0$ and $f=2$, respectively: total-site parity cannot recover this count statistic. The distinction pursued here is an interacting four-site vacancy implementation with parity-only detection and finite-repulsion calibration.

**[WS22]** J. Wu and B. C. Sanders, *Sparse interferometry for measuring multiphoton collective phase*, Physical Review Research **4**, 023134 (2022). [Article](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevResearch.4.023134) · [arXiv:2205.09780](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.09780). Section IV B, Eq. (66), and Fig. 4 use $2n$ spatial modes and two beam-splitter layers to extract collective-phase information from $n$-photon coincidences; Eqs. (75)–(76) isolate its cosine. This is a precedent for trading spatial modes against interferometer depth. Its coincidence statistic and resource objective differ from the four-mode parity precision bound and the Fourier-and-splitter construction here.

**[P25]** G. Park, H. F. Hofmann, R. Okamoto, and S. Takeuchi, *Entangled measurement for W states*, Science Advances **11**, eadx4180 (2025). [Article](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adx4180) · [Published PDF](https://inspirehep.net/files/a6234c0b753eaa6d4fde27f0d12ddea9). Cyclic-shift symmetry and Fourier interferometry support W-basis measurements, including an experimental three-qubit demonstration. The scheme separates polarizations and uses photon-number-resolving detection; these resources lie outside spin-insensitive total-site parity. This is a direct predecessor for collective cyclic-basis readout, rather than a realization of the passive class bounded here.

**[DBS16]** E. Davis, G. Bentsen, and M. Schleier-Smith, *Approaching the Heisenberg Limit without Single-Particle Detection*, Physical Review Letters **116**, 053601 (2016). [Article](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.116.053601) · [arXiv:1508.04110](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04110). One-axis-twisting readout amplifies an encoded phase and relaxes detector-resolution requirements. The broad principle that interactions improve readout under detection constraints is established; their task is phase estimation with prepared probes.

**[NSH17]** S. P. Nolan, S. S. Szigeti, and S. A. Haine, *Optimal and Robust Quantum Metrology Using Interaction-Based Readouts*, Physical Review Letters **119**, 193601 (2017). [Article](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.119.193601) · [arXiv:1703.10417](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.10417). This work derives conditions for optimal phase estimation and noise-robust interaction-based readout. Its parity condition is a symmetry of the probe, generator, and readout basis; the optimality argument uses the full outcome distribution. It is not a theorem for coarse total-site-parity detection of arbitrary unknown spin inputs.

**[K17]** S. Kitamura, T. Oka, and H. Aoki, *Probing and controlling spin chirality in Mott insulators by circularly polarized laser*, Physical Review B **96**, 014406 (2017). [Article](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.96.014406) · [arXiv:1703.04315v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.04315v2). Section IV, Eqs. (43)–(46), especially Eq. (45), relates circular dichroism to scalar chirality in a half-filled fermionic Hubbard Mott insulator using strong-coupling and linear-response expansions. It establishes an indirect optical probe of static chirality. The present result instead certifies arbitrary-input estimation and a restricted-measurement precision separation for a finite bosonic instrument.

**[NGH25]** G. Nambiar, A. Grankin, and M. Hafezi, *Diagnosing Electronic Phases of Matter Using Photonic Correlation Functions*, Physical Review X **15**, 041020 (2025). [Article](https://doi.org/10.1103/67zs-hqf3) · [arXiv:2410.24215](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.24215). Equation (70) relates an outgoing optical quadrature to a material scattering operator. Section VII, particularly Eq. (96), extracts a lattice scalar-chirality sum using phase-sensitive filtered two-photon quadratures and polarization combinations. This is a precedent for indirect static chirality readout and symmetry-based isolation. Its derivation treats a half-filled Fermi-Hubbard Mott insulator in the regime $t\ll|\omega_L-U|\ll U$; the present instrument instead uses three spinful bosons and a vacancy on four sites, with explicitly calibrated finite-repulsion parity effects.

## Sampling comparison

Use the common $\chi=\mathbf S_1\cdot(\mathbf S_2\times\mathbf S_3)$ normalization and count $n$ original independent input trials. The [passive parity theorem](research/PASSIVE_PARITY_BOUND.md) bounds arbitrary spin-independent passive $U(4)$ mixing of three initially singly occupied modes and one vacuum mode, followed by total-site parity. Independent spin-unitary prerotations, adaptive settings on fresh copies, and classical postprocessing are permitted. The worst-case unbiased variance coefficient is at least $13/32=0.40625$; for arbitrary biased estimators the minimax MSE obeys $\liminf_{n\to\infty}nR_n\ge13/32$, with explicit finite-sample bounds in the proof. This lower bound need not be attained. The exactly calibrated interacting score at $U/J=16$, $Jt=\pi$ has certified worst-case coefficient below $0.328697776905$; see the [certificate](research/CERTIFICATE.md). This is a separation within specified particle, mode, and detector resources, not an optimum over unrestricted collective measurements or apparatus costs.

The [separable benchmark](research/SEPARABLE_BENCHMARK.md) separately proves exact minimax coefficient $9/16=0.5625$ for universally unbiased estimation with fully separable effects on each three-spin copy, including classical adaptation between copies. Six uniformly randomized Pauli-product settings attain it. The attained coefficient is already implicit in [R23], Eq. (11) and note [42], after rescaling its variance bound $3/n$ by $3/16$; the additional result here is the lower bound over the full declared separable-effects class. Its proof applies elementary Cauchy–Schwarz and classical Fisher-information arguments to the product-state chirality bound. Passive interferometric effects need not be separable, so this is a different comparison class.

Applying [N26] to one boson per input with arbitrary correlated qubit spins gives a spectral measurement of $\chi$: the cyclic eigenphases produce scores $0,\pm\sqrt3/4$, hence worst-case variance coefficient $3/16$. This chirality consequence is a deduction here from their theorem, not an explicit claim of that source. It requires access to the count statistic above and supplies a stronger sampling reference outside both the separable-effects and passive-parity classes.

The [extra-mode construction](research/PASSIVE_PARITY_BOUND.md) combines this established Fourier measurement with balanced splitting into additional vacuum modes. Three odd output bits certify three distinct occupied arms; a randomized assignment of Fourier ports to splitter groups makes the informative survival probability uniform. Scoring all trials then gives the derived six-, seven-, eight-, and nine-mode coefficients $3/8$, $27/80$, $27/88$, and $9/32$. These are attained values for that construction, not optimality or minimum-mode claims. In particular, the eight-mode example demonstrates why the interacting separation is stated at fixed four-mode resources.

The same separable-effects class, now allowing biased estimators with uniformly bounded mean-square error, obeys the necessary finite count $N\ge(3/(4\varepsilon)-\pi)^2$ for $0<\varepsilon<1/(16\pi)$; see the [proof](research/SEPARABLE_BENCHMARK.md). This extension uses [GL95]. It does not remove calibration confidence or reference costs from the [resource comparison](research/CALIBRATION_COST.md).

The [rational certificate](research/CERTIFICATE.md) also retains a different feasible score designed for the maximally mixed input, with worst-case coefficient below $0.389156263913$ and mixed-input coefficient in $(0.284895494200,0.284895494206)$. These belong to that score, not to the improved worst-case score above. The controlled-cycle Hadamard test in [R23] has worst-case coefficient $1/4$ in this normalization and requires controlled entangling operations outside both declared comparison classes. These ideal comparisons do not establish pointwise superiority on every input.

The [finite-calibration account](research/FINITE_CALIBRATION.md) includes reference preparations and random calibration error in an unconditional MSE guarantee, with separate preparation-error and response-drift allowances. Its worked reuse counts are sufficient mathematical examples under a stable-channel model, not measured apparatus performance or necessary calibration thresholds. The passive information bound and these calibration constructions use standard statistical inequalities, including [GL95]; no new general estimation principle or exhaustive priority result is claimed.

The return-only four-setting example has mixed-input coefficient approximately $0.8759835$ under its stated allocation. Its reconstruction remains valid for every fixed finite positive repulsion with a suitable pair of durations. Complete-image scoring extracts additional information from the detector record, with the separate loss and calibration conditions in [the noise budget](research/NOISE_BUDGET.md).
