# One-duration chirality readout from complete parity images

The complete parity image supplies several reflection-odd observables at the
same evolution duration. Two opposite fluxes therefore suffice whenever their
calibrated coefficient vectors span the two-dimensional space generated by
scalar chirality and the pair-correlation nuisance. This replaces the second
duration in the return-only reconstruction with information already present
in the image. All input preparations, including rejected or zero-score
outcomes, count toward the sample size.

The model, spin normalization, and reflection assumptions are those of
[the main theory](../THEORY.md). The statements below concern terminal
expectation estimation on independent input copies; their proofs do not
assume a coherent retained spin state.

## 1. Effects, physical reflection, and score convention

Write $u=U/J$, $\tau=Jt$, and fix the first flux at $\Phi=-2\pi/3$.
Let $Q_p$ be the total-site-parity projector for the four-bit string $p$, and
define

$$
E_p=V^\dagger e^{i\tau H_{u,\Phi}}Q_p e^{-i\tau H_{u,\Phi}}V.
$$

Here $H$ is dimensionless and $V$ embeds the three input spins at sites
$1,2,3$, with site $0$ empty. At exactly three particles, the possible parity
strings form $\mathcal P=\{p\in\{0,1\}^4:|p|=1\text{ or }3\}$.
Their eight effects obey $E_p\ge0$ and $\sum_pE_p=I_8$.

Let $r(p_0,p_1,p_2,p_3)=(p_0,p_3,p_2,p_1)$ and $R=P_{13}$.
Reflection reverses the flux, so the effect at flux $+2\pi/3$ for the
**physical outcome $r(p)$** is $RE_pR$. Relabeling that outcome is essential;
comparing the same unreflected bit string generally gives a different effect.
Define

$$
O_p=\frac{E_p-RE_pR}{2},\qquad B_p=\frac{E_p+RE_pR}{2}.
$$

On every independent preparation, choose either flux with probability $1/2$.
At flux $-2\pi/3$, report $X=a_p$ on physical pattern $p$; at flux
$+2\pi/3$, report $X=-a_p$ on physical pattern $r(p)$. Report zero for
the eight even-weight parity strings, which are impossible in the ideal
three-particle model. The estimator is $\widehat\chi=n^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^nX_k$.

## 2. Exact reconstruction theorem

Set $\chi=\mathbf S_1\cdot(\mathbf S_2\times\mathbf S_3)$,
$\mathbf S=\boldsymbol\sigma/2$, and $D=P_{12}-P_{23}$. Then

$$
O_p=c_p\chi+d_pD,\qquad
c_p=\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(\chi O_p)}{3/4},\quad
d_p=\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(D O_p)}{12}.
$$

**Proof.** Spin-independent evolution and each spin-blind parity projector
commute with collective spin rotations. Their compressed effects belong to
the three-qubit collective-spin commutant, whose Hermitian span is
$I,P_{12},P_{23},P_{13},\chi$. Conjugation by $R$ exchanges $P_{12}$ and
$P_{23}$ and changes the sign of $\chi$. Its odd part is consequently
$\operatorname{span}\{\chi,D\}$. These two operators are Hilbert–Schmidt
orthogonal, with squared norms $3/4$ and $12$, giving the formulas.

Let $A$ be the $2\times8$ matrix with columns $(c_p,d_p)^T$ and let
$e=(1,0)^T$. The score is unbiased for every input state if and only if

$$
Aa=e.
$$

Indeed, its first-moment operator is
$\sum_pa_pO_p=(Aa)_1\chi+(Aa)_2D$.
The equality is an operator identity and remains valid when the input is
entangled with a reference. The necessary and sufficient condition for an
unbiased score is $e\in\operatorname{range}A$; rank two is a convenient
sufficient condition. Rank one can also suffice when the nuisance vanishes.

Completeness gives $A\mathbf1=0$. Adding any constant to all eight scores
therefore preserves unbiasedness. This freedom is useful for variance
reduction and does not discard any outcomes.

## 3. Minimum variance at the maximally mixed input

Let $q_p=\operatorname{Tr}(E_p)/8$ and $Q=\operatorname{diag}(q_p)$.
Remove any zero-probability outcome: positivity then implies $E_p=0$,
so that outcome contains no reconstruction information. Assume $A$ has rank
two on the remaining support and define $G=AQ^{-1}A^T$. The unique
minimum-second-moment score is

$$
\boxed{a_*=Q^{-1}A^TG^{-1}e,\qquad
n\operatorname{Var}_{I_8/8}(\widehat\chi)=e^TG^{-1}e.}
$$

**Proof.** At this input, the mean is zero and the second moment is
$a^TQa$. Lagrange multipliers for $Aa=e$ give $Qa=A^T\lambda$ and
$G\lambda=e$. Positivity of $Q$ proves strict convexity and uniqueness.
For any feasible $a_*+z$, $Az=0$, and the excess cost is exactly $z^TQz$.

This also optimizes over arbitrary real scores on the same equally
randomized two-flux measurement, not just scores initially constrained to be
antisymmetric. Decompose arbitrary paired scores into an even part $s_p$
and an odd part $a_p$. Unbiasedness requires separately
$\sum_ps_pB_p=0$ and $Aa=e$, whereas their mixed-input second moment is
$\sum_pq_p(s_p^2+a_p^2)$. Setting $s=0$ preserves feasibility and removes
a nonnegative cost. This statement concerns calibrated unbiased linear
estimators for this fixed measurement, not arbitrary physical measurements.

Moreover $q^Ta_*=\mathbf1^TA^TG^{-1}e=0$. Consequently the displayed
mixed-input variance is unchanged if exactly $n/2$ trials are allocated
to each flux, using one half of the difference of the two sample means.
For general inputs fixed equal allocation has variance coefficient

$$
\operatorname{Tr}(T\rho)-\langle\chi\rangle_\rho^2
-\left\langle\sum_pa_pB_p\right\rangle_\rho^2,
\qquad T=\sum_pa_p^2B_p.
$$

The randomized protocol instead has coefficient
$\operatorname{Tr}(T\rho)-\langle\chi\rangle_\rho^2$.

## 4. Exact worst-case variance from three scalar blocks

For the randomized protocol and any unbiased score,

$$
\boxed{\sup_\rho n\operatorname{Var}_\rho(\widehat\chi)=\|T\|.}
$$

This is an equality, not only a second-moment upper bound.
Each $B_p$ commutes with collective rotations and $R$. The input space
decomposes into the spin-$3/2$ symmetric quartet and two spin-$1/2$
doublets distinguished by the reflection eigenvalue. With

$$
\Pi_{\rm s}=\frac{P_{12}+P_{23}+P_{13}}3,\qquad
\Pi_{{\rm m},\pm}=(I-\Pi_{\rm s})\frac{I\pm R}{2},
$$

the positive second-moment operator is
$T=t_{\rm s}\Pi_{\rm s}+t_+\Pi_{{\rm m},+}+t_-\Pi_{{\rm m},-}$.
These projectors have ranks $4,2,2$ and
$\|T\|=\max\{t_{\rm s},t_+,t_-\}$.

**Proof of attainment.** Choose a state supported on a block attaining
the largest eigenvalue. It has definite reflection parity, so its
expectation of the reflection-odd $\chi$ is zero. Its variance therefore
equals the largest eigenvalue. Fixed equal allocation retains $\|T\|$
as an upper bound, but need not attain it because of its extra subtraction.

The mixed-input optimum need not minimize $\|T\|$. If worst-case variance
is the design objective, minimize the maximum of the three positive
quadratic forms $t_j=\sum_pa_p^2\operatorname{Tr}(\Pi_jB_p)/\operatorname{rank}\Pi_j$
subject to $Aa=e$. The [certificate](CERTIFICATE.md) supplies a feasible
score designed for this objective, separately from the mixed-input score;
it does not assert global minimax optimality of the numerical design.

## 5. Two-outcome rank certificate and deterministic stability

For any pair of outcomes $p,q$, let
$\Delta_{pq}=c_pd_q-c_qd_p$. If $\Delta_{pq}\ne0$, a simple feasible
score has $a_p=d_q/\Delta_{pq}$, $a_q=-d_p/\Delta_{pq}$ and all other
scores zero. Thus a single nonzero minor certifies exact one-duration
reconstruction. Using every outcome in the weighted solution can improve
variance substantially over this sparse certificate.

An enclosure of the minor certifies a whole finite neighborhood. Suppose
$|\delta c_j|\le\epsilon_{c,j}$ and
$|\delta d_j|\le\epsilon_{d,j}$ for $j=p,q$. Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
|\delta\Delta_{pq}|\le{}&
|d_q|\epsilon_{c,p}+|c_p|\epsilon_{d,q}
+|d_p|\epsilon_{c,q}+|c_q|\epsilon_{d,p}\\
&+\epsilon_{c,p}\epsilon_{d,q}
+\epsilon_{c,q}\epsilon_{d,p}.
\end{aligned}
$$

If this upper bound is smaller than a certified lower bound on
$|\Delta_{pq}|$, the rank condition persists throughout that neighborhood.

For uniform-ring parameter changes, each parity effect obeys the useful
operator-norm envelope

$$
\|\delta E_p\|\le b,
\quad b=6|\delta\tau|+\frac32\tau_{\max}
\bigl(|\delta u|+|\delta\Phi|\bigr).
$$

**Proof.** Write $H=T+uW$. The hopping bound is $\|T\|\le6$,
$\|\partial_\Phi T\|\le3/2$, and $W$ has spectrum $0,1,3$.
For a Hermitian $L$ and projector $Q$, the off-diagonal block form gives
$\|[L,Q]\|\le(\lambda_{\max}(L)-\lambda_{\min}(L))/2$.
The time derivative contains $[H,Q_p]=[T,Q_p]$, bounded by $6$.
Duhamel's formula for a parameter derivative integrates commutators with
unitarily rotated parity projectors. Centering $W$ at $3I/2$ bounds the
$u$ derivative by $3\tau/2$, and the flux derivative has the same bound.
Integrating along a parameter path proves the envelope.

When the paired fluxes continue to obey reflection symmetry, the same
$b$ bounds $\|\delta O_p\|$. Trace duality gives

$$
|\delta c_p|\le\frac4{\sqrt3}b,\qquad
|\delta d_p|\le\frac1{\sqrt3}b.
$$

Here $\|\chi\|_1=\sqrt3$ and $\|D\|_1=4\sqrt3$.
These bounds establish deterministic local stability; their conservative
constants do not by themselves establish a useful laboratory tolerance.
Keeping the nominal score fixed gives an all-input bias bound
$\|\sum_pa_p\delta O_p\|\le\sum_p|a_p|b_p$.
Recalibration restores the exact identity wherever the rank condition
persists. Uncontrolled reflection-breaking errors require an operator
residual budget, not a claim that the two-observable identity still holds.

## 6. Numerical example and physical scope

At $u=16,\tau=\pi$, the [integer/rational certificate](CERTIFICATE.md)
constructs a worst-case-oriented score with $\|T\|<0.328697776905$.
Its defining rational coordinates and exact two-coordinate correction are
recorded in the certificate. The [passive parity theorem](PASSIVE_PARITY_BOUND.md)
proves the lower bound $13/32$ for the declared four-mode passive class,
and finite minimax inequalities that permit biased and adaptive estimators.
This score and the following mixed-input score solve different design
problems; their error budgets are tracked separately.
Its [parameter-robustness proof](PARAMETER_ROBUSTNESS.md) supplies explicit
primary-score neighborhoods under exact response calibration and a distinct
finite-MSE bound for fixed nominal scores under symmetry-breaking errors.

At $u=16,\tau=\pi$, [the finite-model implementation](complete_parity.py)
gives mixed-input coefficient $0.28489549420$ and randomized worst-case
coefficient $0.38915626391$ for the mixed-input optimal score. Those decimals
are floating-point diagnostics.

For the mixed-input objective, the independent certificate starts
from exact rational reference scores and applies an exact two-outcome
calibration correction. This feasible score is certified to satisfy
$\|T\|<0.389156263913$, with mixed-input coefficient in
$(0.284895494200,0.284895494206)$. The certificate includes all propagator,
constant, and rounding errors, and proves sufficient effect and parameter
neighborhoods after recalibration. Exact mixed-input optimality is a property
of the Gram-formula score; it is not asserted for the nearby certified score.

The [separable-measurement theorem](SEPARABLE_BENCHMARK.md) proves that $9/16$
is the exact minimax variance coefficient for universally unbiased estimation
using fully separable effects on each three-spin copy, including classical
adaptation between copies. Thus the certified all-input coefficient gives a
strict minimax sampling advantage within that comparison. It does not imply
pointwise superiority on every input, an advantage over collective or entangling
measurements, or equal apparatus cost. The controlled-cycle Hadamard-test
bound is $1/4$. The earlier four-setting return estimator has mixed-input
coefficient $0.8759834953$ at its stated parameter pair.

Singleton parity patterns contain useful collision information, but a
one-particle output can mimic them. The complete image still rejects every
single-loss event automatically: $N=2$ has even parity weight, whereas the
ideal $N=3$ outcomes have odd weight. Two lost atoms can contaminate
singleton scores. The [noise budget](NOISE_BUDGET.md) treats this distinction,
survival normalization, parity-bit confusion, and calibration errors.
No successful-trial postselection is used in the estimator or variance.

For a stable spin-invariant, reflection-related noisy instrument, [direct channel calibration](CHANNEL_CALIBRATION.md) reconstructs its complete response from three product references. This route includes lower-particle-number aliases and detector confusion in the calibrated effect itself. Exact coefficients give exact unbiasedness; independent validation controls finite-calibration error. The worst-case-oriented score retains $\|T\|<0.366461$ under exact full-channel recalibration throughout the specified noise window. A separate [finite polynomial calibration](FINITE_CALIBRATION.md), using the analytical window as prior information, supplies an unconditional risk and total-preparation comparison from two product-reference types.

This result changes the terminal use of recorded parity images. The
return event's retained-state operation and its coherence requirements
remain those of the main theory. The collective-spin commutant and
chirality-to-counts predecessors retain the attribution in
[the references](../REFERENCES.md).
