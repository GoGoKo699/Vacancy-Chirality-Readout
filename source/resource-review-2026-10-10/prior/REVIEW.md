## What changed

The finite-U obstacle is now an operator-level question with an answer. The accepted map approaches the ideal filter uniformly over arbitrary and reference-entangled inputs. The proof distinguishes O((J/U)^2) instantaneous leakage probability from O(J/U) coherent error over a fixed hopping time. Postselection error depends on the actual success probability; repeated-return error depends on the round count.

For the uniform ring, finite repulsion produces exactly one contaminating observable in the flux-reversed return contrast: P12−P23. Collective spin symmetry and spatial reflection prove this restriction. A second calibrated duration removes it by a two-by-two classical inversion whenever the coefficient matrix is nonsingular. An exact short-time coefficient calculation proves that suitable two-duration choices exist for every fixed finite positive U/J; it does not make the small-time setting statistically efficient or guarantee a chosen order-one pair of times for every coupling. The U/J=16, Jt=pi and 3pi/4 example is verified numerically with complete matrices and independent exponential calculations. It uses four settings rather than two and does not repair the quantum back-action.

Repeated filtering can generate an almost fully selected chirality sector at finite repulsion, but successful-trial probability no longer has the ideal infinite-round plateau. At U/J=16, four successful returns give approximately 99.91% selected-sector weight and 11.42% unconditional success. These are numerical diagnostics, not a finite-U hardware threshold or device prediction.
